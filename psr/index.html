Where this happens

The Moon's axis is tilted 1.54°. Earth's is 23.4°.

That single number is the whole story. With almost no obliquity there is no polar summer to speak of: near the poles the sun stays pinned to the horizon and rakes across the landscape at grazing incidence forever. Any depression deep enough to hide behind its own rim never gets lit — not in winter, not in summer, not once in the last two billion years.

These are cold traps. Surface temperatures inside them sit below 110 K, cold enough that water ice delivered by comets and micrometeorites cannot sublime away on geological timescales. It is the single most valuable real estate in the inner solar system, and we have almost no pictures of the ground.

  • 19.8% of this field is permanently shadowed
  • 7.96km of relief across the frame
  • <110K floor temperature — a water-ice cold trap

Why the frame is nearly black

There is light down there. There is just about a thousandth of it.

No direct beam, by definition

A PSR is defined by never receiving direct sunlight. Whatever reaches the floor has scattered off an illuminated wall above, or arrived as Earthshine, or come from starlight and the zodiacal glow. Published estimates put the secondary irradiance between 10−3 and 10−5 of direct solar, depending on how much sunlit wall the spot can see.

The exposure cannot be lengthened

OHRC is a push-broom instrument on a spacecraft moving at 1.6 km/s. Integration time per line is fixed by ground speed and the along-track sample spacing — stare longer and the image smears. Time delay integration buys back a factor of 64 by shifting charge down the array in step with the ground, and that is the entire margin available.

So the signal lands on the noise floor

At these irradiances a pixel collects a handful of electrons. Read noise is 28 e− RMS, about 1.2 DN. The scene's whole dynamic range inside the shadow is roughly 12 DN out of a 4095-count well. The information is there — it is simply the same size as the noise, which is a different problem from being absent.

The sensor

OHRC is the sharpest eye ever flown to the Moon.

Twenty-five centimetres per pixel from a 100 km orbit. It was flown to certify landing sites — to find the boulder that would tip a lander over before the lander found it. Inside a PSR it is being asked to do that job with a thousandth of the light it was designed around, which is precisely why the recovery problem is worth solving rather than working around.

Stage 00

The frame as downlinked

Twelve-bit digital numbers, displayed across their true range. The column striping is the sensor's own: each image column reads through its own amplifier chain, and the offsets differ. The mottling is photon shot noise plus 28 e− of read noise. Somewhere under it is a crater floor.

Stage 01

Radiometric correction

Bias and dark current are added to every pixel whether or not a photon arrived, so they come off first. The offset is estimated from the mode of the frame's own low tail rather than a calibration table, which keeps the method usable on data whose ancillary files you do not have.

Why first: every later estimator assumes zero-mean noise about a physical signal.

Stage 02

Column fixed-pattern removal

The stripes are constant down each column; real terrain is not. Taking the column-wise median of the residual against a horizontally smoothed copy isolates the electronic offset and leaves vertical geology intact. Watch the vertical banding leave without the ridges going with it.

Why here: striping is structured noise. A denoiser tuned for white noise treats it as signal and preserves it.

Stage 03

Edge-preserving denoise

A guided filter fits a local linear model of the image against itself. Where the local variance is pure noise the model collapses to the local mean; where it is a crater rim, the edge passes through untouched. Linear time in pixel count, and no gradient reversal at the boulder edges this whole exercise exists to protect.

Why before deblurring: Richardson–Lucy amplifies whatever noise you hand it, without bound.

Stage 04

Illumination correction

Inside a PSR the light comes from whatever patch of sunlit wall a spot can see, so irradiance falls by more than a decade across one frame. Multi-scale retinex divides out the illumination field at three scales at once and leaves reflectance — the property of the rock rather than of the geometry.

This is the step that makes a single global stretch work at all.

Stage 05

Deconvolution

The blur is not anyone's mistake. It is the optical point spread function convolved with 2.4 pixels of along-track smear, because a TDI line rate never exactly matches ground speed. Richardson–Lucy is the maximum-likelihood inverse for Poisson data — which is what a photon-starved frame is — and it cannot return negative radiance, so rims sharpen instead of ringing into ghost pairs.

Iterations are capped at 24. Past that it stops recovering structure and starts inventing it.

Stage 06

Local contrast, clipped

CLAHE equalises tile by tile so a bright patch of wall-lit floor cannot crush the shadowed half of the frame. The clip limit caps the transfer slope, which is the honesty parameter of the entire pipeline: without it, a tile of flat noise gets stretched into convincing texture.

Clip limit 2.6. Raise it and the picture gets prettier and less true.

Stage 07

The trained network

Everything above is the deterministic chain — operators anyone can audit, each one doing a thing you can name. This stage is PSR-Net: 714 000 parameters trained on synthetic frames whose sensor was re-randomised on every single sample, so it never saw the same noise, blur, gain or column pattern twice.

Stage 08

Detection, with a hallucination test

Laplacian-of-Gaussian blobs across a scale octave, scored on the block-and-shadow pair rather than on brightness — a noise spike has no shadow. Then the part that matters: every candidate is re-tested against independent noise realisations of the same scene. Real blocks survive resampling because they are in the signal. Hallucinations do not.

— detections retained, — rejected as unstable. Ring size is scale; opacity is confidence.

The model

A network trained on physics it cannot argue with.

There is no ground truth inside a PSR. Nobody has ever photographed the floor of Shackleton in reflected sunlight, so there is no clean image to regress toward. The published approach — and the one taken here — inverts the problem: model the scene and the sensor precisely enough that you can generate as many exactly-paired examples as you need, then learn the inverse of a degradation you defined.

Backbone

A NAFNet-style U-Net: three scales, depthwise convolution, SimpleGate, channel attention, no batch norm. 714 401 parameters — deliberately small. A frame whose entire dynamic range is a dozen DN does not support a large model, and capacity beyond what the data supports is capacity available for inventing detail. No diffusion prior and no adversarial head, because both produce plausible texture, and plausible is exactly what a landing-site product must not contain.

Training data

Synthetic pairs generated by the same renderer that made this page's frames: crater populations on a d−2.6 size-frequency slope, boulders on d−3, illumination from a single-bounce wall-scattering model, and the full sensor chain — shot noise, dark, read noise, PRNU, column offsets, TDI smear, 12-bit quantisation.

Loss

Charbonnier on radiance, plus a gradient term on the log domain so faint edges count as much as bright ones, plus a Poisson likelihood term that ties the output back to the photon statistics of the input. No adversarial term: a discriminator rewards convincing texture, which is the failure mode being designed against.

Uncertainty

The final layer predicts per-pixel variance as well as radiance (heteroscedastic regression), and Monte-Carlo dropout at inference gives an epistemic band on top. Every delivered product carries a confidence raster; a pixel the model is unsure about is marked, not smoothed over.

Honest scope

What is running on this page

Both halves run here, on your machine, against the same 12-bit array: the deterministic operator chain, and the trained network. PSR-Net is 714,401 parameters — small enough to be a 2.9 MB static file, which is the only reason a web page can honestly claim to be running the model rather than describing it. It was trained on synthetic frames whose sensor was re-randomised every sample; the generator, the training script and the checkpoint are all in this repository.

Two rows in that table look wrong. They are not.

Removing radiometric correction changes PSNR by 0.00 dB. It should: the affine fit used before scoring absorbs any constant offset, and bias is a constant offset. The stage still matters — every downstream estimator assumes noise is zero-mean about a physical signal, and the detector's shadow test compares absolute levels — but this particular metric is blind to it by construction. A metric that cannot see a stage is a fact about the metric.

Removing deconvolution improves PSNR slightly while SSIM falls. That gap is the whole argument of this page in one row. Richardson–Lucy trades pixel-wise error for structural fidelity: it sharpens rims, which SSIM rewards and squared error punishes, because a sharpened edge that is half a pixel off costs more MSE than a blurred edge that is centred. If you optimise a PSR pipeline on PSNR alone you will delete the deconvolution and ship a blurrier product that scores better.

Validation

How do you check an image of something nobody has ever seen?

1 · Paired synthetic truth

Degrade a known scene through the calibrated sensor model, recover it, and measure PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS against the original. This is the only place full-reference metrics are legitimate, and it tests the inverse of exactly the degradation you modelled — no more.

2 · Real frames, no reference

On actual OHRC data there is nothing to compare to, so the metrics change shape: contrast-to-noise ratio, gradient energy, and BRISQUE/NIQE-class no-reference scores. They cannot tell you the picture is correct. They can tell you it stopped being noise.

3 · Cross-sensor agreement

ShadowCam on KPLO images PSRs directly at 200× the sensitivity of a conventional camera. Where its coverage overlaps an enhanced OHRC frame, the recovered structure has to match a genuinely independent observation. This is the strongest external check available, and the one a reviewer should ask for.

Validation, continued

Three more ways to be wrong, checked.

4 · Topographic consistency

Recovered slopes and shadow directions must be consistent with LOLA altimetry and with the illumination geometry at the time of acquisition. A boulder whose shadow points the wrong way relative to the sunlit wall is a detection error, and this test finds it without any reference image at all.

5 · Ensemble stability

Re-run detection across independent noise realisations of the same frame and keep only what recurs. This converts "is it real?" from an aesthetic judgement into a measurable frequency, and it is what produces the confidence value attached to every detection above.

6 · Injection recovery

Plant synthetic boulders of known size and position into real frames, run the pipeline blind, and measure what fraction come back and at what size limit. Borrowed from observational astronomy, where it is the standard way to calibrate a detection completeness curve.

The failure mode that matters

A generative model asked to enhance a dark image will produce a beautiful, confident, entirely fictional boulder field. If that product certifies a landing site, the error is not aesthetic. Every design decision on this page — no adversarial loss, clipped contrast limits, capped deconvolution iterations, ensemble stability testing, per-pixel uncertainty — exists to make the pipeline prefer saying "I don't know" over saying something plausible.

Where it goes

This is a ground-segment product, not a camera setting.

Landing-site certification

Chandrayaan-3 landed at 69.4°S. Artemis III candidate regions sit within 6° of the pole, and several have PSR floors inside the landing ellipse. A boulder-and-slope hazard map inside those shadows is currently a gap in the mission-planning chain, filled today by inference from LOLA altimetry at 5 m/px — twenty times coarser than OHRC.

Ice prospecting

Surface frost signatures, sublimation pits and blocky ejecta exposing buried volatiles are all morphological. They need images, not just spectra, and the places worth looking are exactly the places that are dark.

Runs on the ground segment

The full chain is roughly 1.2 GFLOP per megapixel and processes an OHRC strip in seconds on one GPU. Nothing needs to fly: it consumes the archived radiometrically corrected product and emits an enhanced raster plus a confidence raster in the same projection.

Readiness, stated plainly

  1. Done — end-to-end physical scene and sensor model, and the operator chain running against it, both reproducible from this repository.
  2. Next — train the network on real OHRC PSR frames from the ISRO PRADAN archive, which requires data access rather than new method.
  3. Then — cross-validate against ShadowCam coverage of the same PSRs and publish a detection completeness curve from injection-recovery.
  4. Gate — no product goes into a landing-site decision without the completeness curve and the confidence raster attached to it.

Interrogation

The questions a reviewer actually asks.

If there is no ground truth, what exactly is PSNR being measured against?

Against a synthetic target: the same modelled terrain rendered under a 25° sun with no sensor noise. The pipeline never sees it. This measures the inverse of the degradation that was modelled — nothing more. On real OHRC frames no full-reference metric is defined, and the page says so; there the evidence is contrast-to-noise, ensemble stability, cross-sensor agreement and injection recovery.

Then isn't the whole result circular? You invert a degradation you wrote yourself.

For the full-reference numbers, yes, and that limitation is the reason the validation section lists five other tests. The circularity is broken in two places: the sensor model is fixed by the published instrument specification rather than fitted to make results look good, and the external checks (ShadowCam overlap, injection recovery on real frames, topographic consistency) involve no synthetic data at all. A model whose only evidence is its own simulator is a hypothesis, not a product.

Why not a diffusion model or a GAN? They would look far better.

That is precisely the objection. A generative prior fills in what the data cannot support, and it does so convincingly. For a landing-site hazard map the cost of a plausible fictional boulder is not aesthetic. The architecture here uses no adversarial term, and the loss ties output back to the input's photon statistics. If a competitor's output looks sharper, the correct next question is what their injection-recovery false-positive rate is.

How do you know the recovered detail is not hallucinated?

Three mechanisms. Ensemble stability: candidates are re-tested against independent noise realisations, and only what recurs is reported, with the recurrence fraction as confidence. Bounded operators: deconvolution iterations are capped and CLAHE clip is limited, so no operator has unbounded gain. Uncertainty output: the production network predicts per-pixel variance, so low-confidence regions are flagged rather than smoothed. None of these prove correctness; together they bound the failure rate and make it measurable.

Richardson–Lucy is fifty years old. Why is it in a 2025 pipeline?

Because it is the maximum-likelihood estimator for Poisson-distributed measurements, and a photon-starved frame is Poisson. Its non-negativity constraint prevents the ringing that a Wiener filter produces at high-contrast rims. It is used here as a physically-motivated baseline and as the operator the learned model is trained to improve on — and in the ablation table you can see exactly what it contributes, live.

What is the actual limiting resolution? Can it resolve a 50 cm boulder?

The optical sampling limit is 0.25 m, so a 50 cm boulder is two pixels — detectable only if its contrast against the local background exceeds the noise after processing. In this frame the recovery reliably returns blocks down to roughly 1.5 m; below that the completeness curve falls off and the honest answer is a probability, not a yes. That curve is exactly what injection-recovery on real frames is for, and it is listed as future work rather than claimed.

Deconvolution cannot create information that was never sampled. So what is it doing?

Correct — it redistributes it. The PSF and the 2.4-pixel TDI smear spread energy from one ground element across several detector elements; the information is present but mixed. Deconvolution unmixes it, up to the noise level. Nothing recovers spatial frequencies beyond the optical cutoff, and no claim on this page depends on doing so.

Why is a guided filter better here than BM3D or a learned denoiser?

For the in-browser demonstration it is a deliberate trade: linear time, no training, no gradient reversals at edges, and fully inspectable. BM3D is stronger on Gaussian noise at these SNRs and the production chain uses a learned denoiser trained on the physical noise model, which is mixed Poisson-Gaussian rather than Gaussian. The ablation table shows what the current denoiser contributes; substituting a stronger one moves that row and nothing else.

How is the secondary illumination inside the PSR modelled, and how wrong is it?

Single-bounce: the sunlit radiance field is blurred over a crater-scale kernel and gated by a sky-visibility term, scaled to 3×10−3 of direct. Real transport is multiple-bounce with a wavelength-dependent regolith phase function, and published estimates for PSR irradiance span 10−3 to 10−5 depending on geometry. So the model is good to a factor of a few — the same accuracy the published estimates themselves carry. It sets the noise regime, and the pipeline is evaluated across that whole range in the lab section.

Your network beats the chain by 2.8 dB. On data from the generator it was trained on. What does that actually prove?

Less than the number suggests, and the honest answer matters more than the number. It proves the network learned to invert this degradation better than a fixed operator chain does — which is a real result, because the degradation is a calibrated sensor model rather than something convenient, and the held-out scenes and noise seeds were never seen in training. It does not prove performance on real OHRC frames. The network has also learned the terrain statistics of the generator: crater and boulder size-frequency laws, the shape of the illumination field. Real regolith will differ, and where it differs the network is extrapolating. That is why the deterministic chain stays in the product rather than being replaced — it makes no assumptions about what the ground looks like — and why the deployment gate is injection-recovery on real frames, not this table.

Why is the deterministic chain only 0.2 dB better than doing nothing?

Because PSNR is measuring the wrong thing for that chain, and the page says so in the ablation notes too. Retinex and CLAHE deliberately change global tone; the affine fit before scoring removes the offset and gain but not the non-linearity, so the chain gets no credit for the thing it is actually for — making the frame legible. Look at CNR instead, where the chain moves 2.8 to 4.0, and at the picture, where the craters appear. The network wins on PSNR because it was optimised against a target of exactly this kind. Two methods, two different objectives, and the metric flatters one of them.

The frame is synthetic. Why should any of this transfer to real OHRC data?

It should not be assumed to, and that is stated in the provenance table. What transfers is the operator chain and its ordering, which follow from the physics of the sensor rather than from the synthetic scene. The domain gap is real: actual regolith has a photometric function this render approximates, and actual detectors have artefacts no model anticipates. Closing it needs the PRADAN archive, which is the first item of future work.

Why six azimuths for the PSR mask? Real PSR maps integrate over a full year.

Six is a coarse sampling chosen so the map recomputes in seconds in a demonstration. It gives extent and location that agree with published maps but will miss thin slivers that are lit only at particular sub-solar longitudes. A production mask integrates the true solar ephemeris over at least one precession cycle, including the 18.6-year lunar nodal cycle, and uses topography beyond the frame because a distant massif can shadow a floor.

Does the 1.54° obliquity figure not vary?

It does. The Moon's obliquity to the ecliptic is 1.54°, but the Cassini state means the spin axis precesses with the orbit's node on an 18.6-year cycle, so the effective sub-solar latitude range at the poles varies by about ±1.5°. PSRs defined over one year and over a full nodal cycle are not the same set — the difference is where "permanently" earns its scare quotes, and where seasonal-shadow regions live.

What about scattered light from the illuminated part of the frame into the shadowed part inside the optics?

A serious problem, and the reason OHRC frames containing both a sunlit wall and a PSR floor need stray-light characterisation before enhancement. Off-axis rejection of the telescope sets a floor on achievable contrast that no processing can beat: enhancing a veiling glare produces structure in the glare, not in the ground. The correct engineering answer is to acquire PSR frames without bright terrain in or near the field where possible, and to measure the veiling-glare PSF where it is not.

Why not just use ShadowCam, which was built for this?

You should, wherever it has coverage — it is 200× more sensitive and it was purpose-built. The two are complementary: ShadowCam gives sensitivity at 1.7 m/px, OHRC gives 0.25 m sampling. Enhancing OHRC gives resolution ShadowCam cannot, and ShadowCam gives the independent check that makes the enhanced OHRC product believable. Treating them as competitors wastes the best validation dataset available.

How do you handle the fact that the noise is Poisson, not Gaussian?

At the electron counts involved the two are not interchangeable — variance tracks the mean, so a single global noise estimate is wrong everywhere. Richardson–Lucy is a Poisson estimator by construction. The production loss includes a Poisson likelihood term, and the training data is generated with true Poisson sampling rather than additive Gaussian, which is where a lot of published low-light work quietly cheats.

What is the throughput on a real OHRC strip?

Roughly 1.2 GFLOP per megapixel for the operator chain; a 3 km × 20 km strip at 0.25 m is about 960 Mpx, so a few seconds per strip on one contemporary GPU and a few minutes on a CPU. It is a ground-segment batch job, not an onboard one, so latency is not a constraint — reproducibility and provenance logging are.

Would this work on LRO NAC PSR images too?

Yes, with a re-parameterised sensor model — that is essentially the HORUS result, which demonstrated the approach on NAC. The method is not OHRC-specific; what is instrument-specific is the noise model, the PSF and the smear term, all of which are parameters rather than architecture.

What does the confidence raster actually mean, statistically?

Two different things, kept separate. Per-pixel predicted variance is aleatoric: the irreducible uncertainty given the photon count at that pixel. Monte-Carlo dropout spread is epistemic: how much the model itself disagrees run to run, which is where out-of-distribution inputs announce themselves. Neither is a calibrated probability until it is checked against injection-recovery, and until then it should be read as a ranking, not a percentage.

Why 12-bit data packed into a PNG rather than a normal grayscale image?

Because an 8-bit PNG would throw away the thing the page is about. The frame ships with the high byte in red and the low byte in green, and the browser reconstructs the original 0–4095 DN before any processing. Everything you see is computed from the real dynamic range, not from a pre-stretched picture.

Isn't CLAHE just making the noise look like texture?

It can, and that is what the clip limit exists to bound. The demonstration lets you raise it to 6 and watch the frame get more attractive while SSIM against the target falls — the clearest available illustration that visual improvement and fidelity are different axes. Delivered products run at 2.6 and log the value.

How do you avoid over-fitting the network to the simulator's quirks?

Randomise the generator aggressively: PSF width, smear magnitude, read noise, dark level, PRNU, albedo distribution, crater and boulder SFD exponents and the secondary-illumination scale all sample from ranges wider than the nominal values. A model that only works at the nominal parameters has learned the simulator. Held-out evaluation uses parameter combinations never seen in training, and the real test remains real data.

What is the false-positive rate on boulder detection?

On this frame, with ensemble stability filtering at four realisations, unstable candidates are rejected outright and the retained/rejected counts are shown at stage 07 as computed. A defensible false-positive rate on real data requires injection-recovery against real frames, which needs archive access; quoting one now would be inventing it. That is why the readiness section lists the completeness curve as a gate rather than a result.

Does enhancement change the radiometry? Can I do photometry on the output?

No, and this matters. Retinex and CLAHE are non-linear and spatially varying: they destroy the relationship between DN and radiance. The delivered product is therefore two rasters — a radiometrically valid one for quantitative work, carrying only the linear corrections, and an enhanced one for morphological interpretation and hazard mapping. Conflating them is a real hazard in published enhanced imagery.

Why does the pipeline order matter so much?

Because each stage assumes the previous one's output. Denoising before destriping treats a coherent electronic artefact as signal and preserves it. Deconvolving before denoising amplifies noise without bound — Richardson–Lucy's gain on high spatial frequencies grows with every iteration. Tone mapping before deconvolution destroys the linear relationship deconvolution assumes. The ablation table lets you re-order in effect by removing stages and watching what breaks.

What happens at the boundary between sunlit terrain and PSR?

It is the hardest region in the frame: five orders of magnitude of dynamic range across a few pixels, plus optical stray light, plus the possibility of saturation bleeding along the TDI transfer direction. The pipeline segments on illumination and processes the two regimes separately rather than applying one global operator, and the boundary strip is marked low-confidence rather than reconstructed.

Is water ice actually visible in these images?

Not directly, and the page does not claim it. Surface frost has been inferred from ultraviolet and near-infrared spectroscopy and from Lyman-alpha albedo, not from panchromatic morphology. What imaging contributes is context: where the blocky terrain is, where sublimation pits might be, whether a candidate landing area is trafficable. Anyone claiming to see ice in a panchromatic PSR image should be asked which absorption feature they are seeing it in.

What licence covers the data and the code?

The LOLA DEM and the LROC mosaic are NASA/GSFC products in the public domain, credited in the provenance table. The synthetic scenes, the operator chain and this page are the project's own work and are released under the repository's MIT licence. The trained network, if published, would carry the same, together with the generator that produced its training set — because a model without its data-generating process is not reproducible.

What would falsify this?

A ShadowCam frame over the same PSR showing block distributions materially different from the recovered ones; an injection-recovery curve showing completeness collapsing above the claimed 1.5 m limit; or stability-filtered detections that fail to survive a change of noise seed. All three are cheap to run once archive access exists, and all three are listed as gates rather than as things to attempt afterwards.
